# Account-access UX audit

Audience: first-time small-team coordinators choosing a plan and requesting account access.

This audit uses only the supplied synthetic design observations and written flow. Frame and component IDs below are supplied locators. No live design, prototype, implementation, or user behavior was inspected. “Not represented” means an evidence gap, not a confirmed missing product feature.

## Prioritized findings

| Priority | Finding and evidence | Recommended change and validation |
|---|---|---|
| P1 — confirmed | **Essential instructions have insufficient text contrast.** `10:8`, “Terms explanation” (D03), uses opaque #9A9A9A on white at 14px normal. The calculated ratio is **2.81:1**, below the 4.5:1 WCAG AA threshold for normal text. Coordinators may miss instructions needed to proceed. | Darken the text to a color meeting at least 4.5:1; verify the final color pair across applicable states. Keep these instructions readable rather than relying on a tooltip or color change alone. |
| P1 — confirmed visual issue | **Field identity disappears while typing.** `10:1`, “Create account / Desktop” (D01), has placeholders without persistent visible email/password labels. This makes review and error recovery harder. Accessible names are unknown. | Add persistent visible labels linked to inputs. Keep any format/password guidance separate from placeholders. Verify accessible names, keyboard order, autocomplete, and password-manager behavior in implementation. |
| P1 — confirmed visual issue; runtime unknown | **The supplied desktop error treatment relies on color alone.** D05 shows only a red field border. Users cannot identify the reason or recovery action from this represented state. Programmatic error handling is unknown. | Add a concise textual error beside the relevant field, explaining the correction; retain entered data. Verify association with the field, announcement when errors appear, and keyboard access to correction. Test invalid email, rejected password, and server failure. |
| P1 — journey risk to validate | **The verification-to-dashboard transition is ambiguous.** F03 shows “Check your email” plus Continue, without a documented verified/unverified outcome. F04 shows “Start here” without a represented next action. A new coordinator may not know whether access is ready or how to begin. | Specify what Continue does before and after verification; provide a clear pending state and successful transition. Give the empty dashboard one concrete primary action, such as “Create your team,” if that matches the intended access model. Test with an unverified account and ask a first-time coordinator to complete the next meaningful task unaided. |
| P1 — commercial clarity risk to validate | **Yearly commitment may be unclear.** F01 defaults to yearly billing while showing per-user/month prices; the artifact does not establish whether an annual total appears before submission. | Show the billing cadence, annual charge, and seat-count basis near the selected plan and final commitment. Make a monthly/yearly switch unambiguous and preserve the selected plan through F02–F03. Verify that users can explain what they will be charged and when before proceeding. |
| P2 — confirmed layout issue; runtime unknown | **Mobile help text exceeds its frame.** `10:2`, “Create account / Mobile” (D02), describes a 38px overflow at 360px width. Runtime clipping and horizontal scrolling are unverified. | Constrain help text to the content width and allow wrapping. Check the rendered form at 320px and 360px widths, with text enlargement and zoom, for readable instructions and no lost content or unwanted horizontal scrolling. |
| P2 — interaction risk to validate | **Closely spaced icons may be hard to activate accurately.** `10:9`, “Close” (D04), has an 18×18px visible icon beside another with a 2px gap. Visible bounds do not establish actual target size or accessibility compliance. | Inspect hit areas before changing the component. Provide distinct, sufficiently large targets with separation; aim for at least 24×24 CSS pixels where the applicable minimum-target criterion requires it, and prefer larger touch areas. Verify accessible names, visible focus, keyboard activation, and the close destination. |
| P2 — recovery/navigation gaps | **The flow does not document key interruption paths.** F02 omits loading, field/network errors, back navigation, and data retention. F03 omits resend timing and change-email behavior. These are gaps in the supplied artifact. | Specify disabled/loading states that prevent duplicate submissions without hiding progress; add recoverable network feedback and retry. Define back behavior and retain safe form data and plan choice. Document resend cooldown/status, change-email routing, and expired-link recovery. Exercise each branch through to success. |

## Hierarchy and consistency

The represented Create account button (D01) supplies an apparent primary action, but the evidence cannot establish its prominence relative to the complete page. F04’s generic heading provides weak task direction in the supplied flow. Use specific action language and one clear primary action per step. Keep plan names, selected billing cadence, account email, and access status consistent across F01–F04. Standardize labels, help text, errors, focus, and loading treatments across desktop and mobile; current cross-screen consistency cannot be assessed from these observations alone.

## Recommended sequence

1. Correct the confirmed contrast, persistent-label, and visual-error issues; repair the mobile help-text layout.
2. Resolve plan commitment and verification/first-action semantics in the flow; define recovery branches.
3. Validate the implemented journey with keyboard, a screen reader, narrow viewports, and first-time coordinators. Check whether they understand billing, recover from an error, verify their email, and reach the first meaningful team action without help.

No usability-test results, runtime accessibility conformance, measured completion rates, or production defects are claimed. Priorities reflect likely task impact from the supplied evidence, not observed frequency.
